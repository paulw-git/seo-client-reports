The owner’s priorities

A damaged building creates financial and operational pressure

Commercial property owners have to think beyond the immediate repair. They are protecting the building’s value, supporting tenants, managing risk, and deciding how quickly the property needs to return to normal use.

01

Protect the asset

A fast, informed response can help limit additional damage and preserve more of the building’s structure, finishes, and systems.

02

Reduce downtime

Every day a commercial property is partially or fully unavailable can affect tenants, revenue, schedules, and customer access.

03

Control the scope

A clear assessment helps separate emergency work, repair needs, code considerations, and longer-term improvements.

04

Preserve the investment

Complete, coordinated repairs help owners protect the property’s condition, usability, and long-term market value.

Commercial restoration support

From emergency response to complete reconstruction

The right plan depends on the building, the source of the damage, the affected tenants, and the owner’s business objectives.

Water and flood damage

Leaks, burst pipes, flooding, and plumbing failures can affect finishes, walls, floors, equipment areas, and neighboring spaces.

Generation Contracting can help owners understand the affected areas and coordinate the repair path from immediate protection through reconstruction.

Fire and smoke restoration

Fire damage often involves more than the area where flames were visible. Smoke, soot, odor, water, and structural concerns can extend the scope.

A complete plan helps identify what can be restored, what needs replacement, and how the building can be returned to service.

Storm and exterior repairs

Severe weather may affect roofing, siding, windows, drainage, interiors, and shared building systems at the same time.

A coordinated review helps prevent visible exterior repairs from overlooking related interior or water-intrusion issues.

Tenant and common-area restoration

Damage may affect leased suites, corridors, lobbies, restrooms, mechanical rooms, parking areas, or other shared spaces.

The work sequence should consider access, tenant schedules, safety, and the owner’s plan for reopening or re-leasing the space.

Mold and environmental concerns

Moisture that is not addressed properly can create additional building concerns and complicate an already difficult repair.

Owners should have the condition evaluated and should confirm the appropriate scope, containment, removal, and repair steps for the property.

Insurance repair and remodeling

Owners may need to coordinate documentation, estimates, approvals, tenants, and contractors during an insurance-related restoration.

Generation Contracting can help stakeholders discuss the scope and the work required to move the property toward recovery.

A clear path forward

What commercial property owners can expect

A strong project begins with a shared understanding of the building, the damage, the priorities, and the decisions that need to be made.

1

Property and damage review

Discuss the building, the apparent cause, affected areas, current occupants, emergency measures, and the owner’s most urgent concerns.

2

Scope and priority planning

Separate immediate protection from repair, reconstruction, code, finish, and operational needs so the next steps are easier to understand.

3

Stakeholder coordination

Identify the people who need to be involved, including owners, tenants, property managers, insurers, building staff, and specialty contractors.

4

Restoration and reconstruction

Complete the approved work with attention to safety, sequencing, site access, workmanship, tenant needs, and the building’s intended use.

5

Completion and return to use

Review remaining items, confirm the repaired areas, and prepare the property for occupancy, tenant operations, leasing, or sale.

The goal is a complete solution, not just a quick patch

Owners need repairs that address the actual condition of the property and support its next stage of use. A coordinated scope can help avoid repeated disruption and incomplete work.
