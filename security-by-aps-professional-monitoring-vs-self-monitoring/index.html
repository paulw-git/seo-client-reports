The short answer

The biggest difference is who is responsible for the next step.

With self-monitoring, alerts go to you or someone on your team and you decide what to do. With professional monitoring, a trained monitoring team is part of the response process according to the plan established for the property.

Self-monitoring

You receive notifications, check video or alarm information, and decide whether to contact a keyholder, staff member, emergency service, or other response resource.

Professional monitoring

A monitoring team helps watch for events and follow the response plan when activity or an alarm requires attention. APS describes its remote video service as providing 24/7 monitoring, alerts, and live operator intervention.

Compare the experience

Five questions that usually make the decision clearer.

Neither approach is automatically right for every home, business, or job site. Start by looking at the practical demands placed on the people responsible for security.

Who is watching?

Self-monitoring depends on an owner, manager, employee, or other contact noticing and acting on an alert. Professional monitoring adds a dedicated monitoring team to the process.

What happens at 2 a.m.?

Consider whether the responsible person can reliably answer notifications during sleep, travel, meetings, weekends, holidays, or a busy shift.

How much context is available?

Video analytics, live video, recorded footage, audio warnings, and a documented response plan can help turn an alert into a more informed decision.

The right question is not only “Can I receive an alert?” It is “Who can make a useful decision when the alert arrives?”
Decision guide

When each approach may be a better fit.

Ask about the response process

Before choosing a provider, ask who receives alerts, how events are verified, what talk-down or dispatch options exist, and how keyholders are contacted.

Buyer questions

Questions to ask before choosing.

Does professional monitoring mean I cannot access my cameras?

Not necessarily. Ask whether authorized owners or managers can still access live video, recorded events, and system controls remotely.

Is self-monitoring always less expensive?

Pricing depends on the equipment, monitoring service, installation, response features, and other terms. Compare the total cost and the responsibility placed on your team rather than assuming one option is always cheaper.

Can professional monitoring reduce false alarms?

Some systems use AI-assisted analytics and video verification to help prioritize activity. Ask how the provider distinguishes people, vehicles, animals, weather, and other causes of alerts.

What should a business include in its response plan?

Define who is contacted, what counts as a verified event, when live audio or dispatch is appropriate, who can access video, and how the plan changes when staff, tenants, or site conditions change.
