The self-storage security challenge

One property can contain a lot of ground to watch.

Self-storage facilities often combine multiple buildings, vehicle gates, loading areas, fencing, and long stretches without staff on site. A useful security plan needs to help operators see what is happening, determine whether activity matters, and respond with the right next step.

Common concerns

Break-ins, vandalism, trespassing, loitering, and attempted theft can happen after hours or in areas that are difficult to monitor from a single office.

What operators need

Operators may need better visibility across the property, fewer nuisance alerts, remote access to video, and a way to support timely response without building a new wired infrastructure.

Self-storage security questions

What facility operators often want to know.

Can a self-storage facility be monitored without hardwired internet?

Some facilities may be candidates for solar-powered, battery-backed, cellular, or other flexible deployment options. A site review is needed to determine what is practical for the property.

Which areas should a storage-facility system cover?

Common priorities include vehicle gates, loading zones, drive aisles, perimeter fencing, building entrances, and other locations where after-hours activity could create risk.

Can a security system help reduce nuisance alerts?

AI-assisted video analytics can help classify activity and prioritize alerts involving people or vehicles. The right configuration depends on the property, camera placement, lighting, and monitoring plan.
