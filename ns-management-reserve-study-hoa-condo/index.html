Reserve Studies for HOA and Condo Associations

A practical guide to understanding the property components, future costs, and funding decisions that shape an association’s long-term financial plan.

A reserve study turns future repairs into a planning conversation. Instead of waiting for a roof, parking lot, elevator, or mechanical system to fail, the board can use a reserve study to understand what the community is responsible for, when major components may need attention, and how much money may need to be set aside over time.

Inventory

The study identifies major common elements the association is responsible for maintaining.

Useful life

It estimates how long each component may last before repair or replacement becomes likely.

Funding plan

It helps the board connect future capital needs with a reasonable reserve contribution strategy.

What a reserve study examines

A professional reserve study generally begins with an inventory of the association’s major shared assets. Depending on the community, that inventory may include roofs, exterior surfaces, roads, parking areas, pools, elevators, fences, boilers, HVAC systems, and other components covered by the association’s responsibilities.

The study then considers each component’s current condition, estimated remaining useful life, expected replacement or repair cost, and projected timing. It is a planning tool rather than a promise that every project will occur on an exact date or at an exact price.

Why the useful-life estimate matters

Every major asset has a different maintenance cycle. A roof may require replacement sooner than a concrete walkway, while an elevator or mechanical system may need specialized evaluation. Understanding these timelines helps the board avoid treating every future expense as if it will happen at once.

The estimates also help the board prioritize inspections, maintenance, and funding decisions. If a component is approaching the end of its expected life, the board may need to obtain updated bids, schedule a professional inspection, or review whether current reserve contributions are sufficient.

How a reserve study guides the budget

The funding recommendations in a reserve study can help the board decide how much should be contributed to reserves over time. The goal is to build a plan that recognizes future capital obligations while considering the current financial position of the association and the effect on homeowner assessments.

A study cannot remove uncertainty. Construction prices, inflation, weather, maintenance quality, and project scope can all change. The value is in giving the board a documented starting point for long-term decisions instead of relying on guesswork.

When should a board review or update it?

Reserve studies should be revisited as the community changes and as major projects are completed or added. Many associations use a multi-year update cycle, but the appropriate timing depends on the property, the original study, governing documents, applicable requirements, and professional guidance.

An update may also be useful when the board sees major cost changes, discovers a component was omitted, changes the scope of a project, or experiences a condition that differs materially from the original assumptions.
