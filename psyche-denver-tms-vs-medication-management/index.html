TMS vs. Medication Management for Depression

TMS and medication management are not always competing choices. A psychiatric provider may discuss one approach, the other, or a coordinated plan based on your needs.

Medication managementTMS therapyDepression care
The short answer

The choice is clinical and personal

Medication management may be part of ongoing psychiatric care. TMS is a non-invasive treatment course that may be considered when depression has not improved enough with medication or when a patient needs another option.

Start here

How the two approaches differ

Medication management uses prescribed medicines with follow-up monitoring and adjustments. TMS uses repeated magnetic stimulation sessions. Neither approach is automatically right for every patient.

Medication management

Medication management involves evaluating symptoms, selecting medication when appropriate, monitoring response and side effects, and adjusting the plan over time.

  • Can be part of long-term care
  • May involve medication changes or combinations
  • Follow-up timing varies by patient
  • Can be coordinated with therapy or other care

TMS therapy

TMS is a non-invasive treatment course delivered in a clinical setting. It does not involve adding a systemic medication to the body.

  • Typically delivered in repeated sessions
  • Patients remain awake during treatment
  • Often considered for treatment-resistant depression
  • Requires an evaluation and treatment schedule

How to think it through

How a provider may think through the decision

1

Review your history

Look at symptoms, diagnoses, previous medications, response, side effects, and current goals.

2

Clarify the problem

Determine whether the main issue is lack of response, tolerability, access, adherence, or another factor.

3

Discuss options

Compare medication changes, TMS, therapy referrals, Spravato, or coordinated care as appropriate.

4

Build a plan

Choose a monitored next step and define how progress will be reviewed.

Common questions

Questions to bring to a provider

Is TMS an alternative to antidepressants?

TMS may be considered as an alternative or an addition in some treatment plans. A provider must evaluate your individual circumstances.

Can medication management continue during TMS?

Some patients continue existing medications during TMS, but your treating provider should make that decision and coordinate any changes.

How do I know if medication has not worked?

Your provider may consider dose, duration, adherence, side effects, diagnosis, and the degree of improvement—not only whether symptoms disappeared completely.
