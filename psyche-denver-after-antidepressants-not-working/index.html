What Treatment May Be Considered After Antidepressants Have Not Worked?

If an antidepressant has not helped enough, the next step is not the same for everyone. A careful review can uncover additional options and clarify what to try next.

Treatment-resistant depressionNext-step carePsychiatric evaluation
The short answer

Start with a full treatment review

A provider may review the diagnosis, dose, duration, adherence, side effects, other medications, medical factors, therapy access, and safety concerns before discussing next-step treatments.

Start here

Several next steps may be considered

The goal is not simply to move to a more advanced treatment. It is to understand why the current plan has not worked well enough and choose a monitored next step.

Revisit medication management

The provider may adjust the dose, switch medications, consider a combination, or review whether another diagnosis or medical factor is affecting response.

  • Review what was tried and for how long
  • Discuss side effects and adherence
  • Consider interactions and medical history
  • Set a clear follow-up plan

Consider a non-medication treatment

TMS may be discussed for some people with depression who have not found enough relief with medication.

  • Non-invasive outpatient treatment
  • Typically delivered over repeated sessions
  • Requires an evaluation and schedule
  • May be coordinated with current care

Discuss Spravato® when appropriate

Spravato is an FDA-approved esketamine treatment for specific depression indications and is administered under medical supervision.

  • Used after clinical evaluation
  • In-office dosing and observation
  • Insurance and authorization may matter
  • Treatment schedule is structured

How to think it through

A practical next-step conversation

1

Bring your history

List medications, doses, duration, benefits, side effects, and why each was stopped or continued.

2

Describe your current symptoms

Explain what remains difficult in sleep, energy, focus, work, relationships, or daily functioning.

3

Ask about options

Discuss medication changes, therapy, TMS, Spravato, referrals, and what evidence applies to your situation.

4

Agree on follow-up

Decide what will be monitored, when you will check in, and what should prompt an earlier call.

Common questions

Questions to bring to a provider

Does one antidepressant failure mean I have treatment-resistant depression?

Not necessarily. Treatment resistance depends on the full clinical history, including diagnosis, adequate trials, response, tolerability, and other factors.

What if an antidepressant helped but stopped working?

Tell your provider. They may review adherence, stressors, interactions, dosage, diagnosis, and other treatment options.

What should I do if I am in crisis?

If you may harm yourself or someone else, call or text 988 in the United States, call 911, or go to the nearest emergency department. Do not wait for a routine appointment.
