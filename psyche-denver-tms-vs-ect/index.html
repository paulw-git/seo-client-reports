TMS vs. ECT: What Is the Difference?

TMS and electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) are both medical treatments that can be discussed for serious depression, but they are very different procedures.

TMS therapyECT comparisonDepression treatment
Important context

This is not a decision to make from a webpage

ECT may be considered in situations that require a different level of intervention. TMS is non-invasive and typically delivered while the patient is awake. A qualified psychiatric team must guide this decision.

TMS and ECT use different treatment methods

The comparison below is educational. It does not determine which treatment is appropriate and should not be used to delay urgent mental-health care.

TMS therapy

TMS uses magnetic pulses to stimulate targeted brain regions. It is delivered in an outpatient setting without general anesthesia.

  • Non-invasive procedure
  • Patient remains awake
  • Usually delivered as a repeated course
  • No routine recovery period from anesthesia

ECT

ECT uses a controlled electrical stimulus while the patient is under general anesthesia. It is performed by a specialized medical team with recovery monitoring.

  • Hospital or specialized clinical setting
  • General anesthesia is used
  • Often considered for severe or urgent conditions
  • Requires recovery planning and medical clearance

Common questions

Questions to bring to a provider

Is TMS less serious than ECT?

They are different medical treatments with different indications, risks, and levels of intervention. The right comparison depends on the patient’s clinical situation.

Does ECT mean other treatments failed?

ECT may be considered for several reasons, including severity or urgency. Only a qualified treatment team can explain why it may be recommended.
